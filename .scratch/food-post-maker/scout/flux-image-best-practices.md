## What it does

It teaches the agent how to write prompts for FLUX image models: prose rather than keyword lists, subject first, lighting stated explicitly, camera and lens terms for photo realism, and positive phrasing because FLUX has no negative prompts. It has a section specific to FLUX.2 [klein] (no prompt upsampling, so be descriptive yourself; narrative prose of 40 to 70 words; front-load the subject; lean on lighting). It reaches nothing: no code, no network calls, no tools.

## Gain for this task

In the food post maker, Claude Haiku writes the FLUX.2 klein 9B prompts at runtime. The developer has to write the Haiku system prompt that tells it how. Nothing installed covers FLUX prompting: `claude-api` covers the Haiku call and structured output, not what makes a good klein prompt. Three points from this skill bear directly on the PRD:

- **No negative prompts.** A Haiku instruction such as "no text, no logos" would backfire. The skill's replacement table ("clean unmarked surfaces", "unbranded", "plain") gives the positive phrasing, which matters for the PRD's no-third-party-brand rule and for keeping garbled lettering out of the pictures.
- **klein does not upsample prompts.** Haiku's prompts need to be full prose of about 40 to 70 words that name the dish first, then the setting and the light, rather than short dish names.
- **Lighting and camera vocabulary** (overhead or 45-degree food shots, soft window light, shallow depth of field, macro detail) gives the prompt template concrete terms to draw on.

The gain is a better system prompt for one server module, `server/food-post-maker.js`, on the first pass. It is modest: a developer could get the same from docs.bfl.ai.
